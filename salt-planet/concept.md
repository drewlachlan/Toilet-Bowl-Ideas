# Novel Worldbuilding Notes

## Core Concept
A tidally-locked planet ("Planet A") orbiting close to its star, built on real "eyeball planet" astrophysics. No magic system — resource scarcity, physics, and human ambition drive the conflict.

## Planetary Setup

- **Tidal lock:** Planet A is tidally locked to its star (ordinary 0° obliquity). One fixed point, **the Eye**, permanently faces the star and is scorching hot. The opposite point, **the Anti-Eye**, is permanent night — cool/cold, but never freezing (no ice).
- **Habitable zone:** a ring around the planet's terminator/equator, between the Eye and Anti-Eye — perpetual horizon-level light, no day/night cycle.
- **The Moon:** a large, close moon orbits pole-to-pole (through the Eye and Anti-Eye) rather than around the equator. *Open question: what keeps this orbit stable over time.*
- The moon's tidal pull is strongest where its ground track crosses the habitable ring, and tapers off gradually around the rest of the ring — a gradient of tidal intensity rather than fixed hot-spots.

## Water Cycle & Geography

- **Single planetary cycle:** cold water flows along the ocean floor from the Anti-Eye toward the Eye. At the Eye, the star's heat evaporates it, leaving trace minerals behind to slowly concentrate. Clouds carry the evaporated water back to the Anti-Eye, where it rains onto cool (never freezing) landmasses.
- The water is fresh, with only trace mineral/salt levels, like a normal river. Rain on the Anti-Eye picks up trace minerals as it runs off, same as any river, and carries them back toward the Eye.
- This cycle has been running long enough for those trace amounts to accumulate into a real, concentrated deposit at the Eye.
- **The Eye's resource:** the accumulated mineral deposit at the Eye is the resource civilizations compete over. *Open question: exactly what makes it so valuable (rarity, unique property, pure economic scarcity).*
- **The Anti-Eye:** currently has no resource of its own — it's the source/collection end of the cycle (rainfall, rivers, mineral pickup), while the Eye is the concentration/payoff end.
- **Tides and tidal bores:** the moon's periodic tidal pull amplifies the baseline current into a dramatic surge. Along the habitable ring, funnel-shaped bays produce Bay of Fundy-style tidal bores — the closer a bay sits to the moon's ground track, the more violent its bore. This creates a natural violence/wealth gradient along the ring.
- **The extraction mechanic:** the poles are otherwise lethal (boiling heat at the Eye, dangerous cold at the Anti-Eye). The periodic surge briefly moderates conditions enough for expeditions to travel in and extract resources before the window closes. The planetary river current only flows in one direction — toward the Eye — so vessels ride that current inbound. For the return trip, the same vessels transform into blimps/balloon airships and ride the return air current (the cloud/vapor cycle flowing back toward the Anti-Eye) home.

## Civilizations

Five, spread along the habitable ring:

1. **Benevolent civilization #1** — details TBD.
2. **Benevolent civilization #2 (the "monks"):** semi-pacifist; capable of defending itself but prefers to hire mercenaries for open conflict rather than fight directly. *Open question: their (non-magical) reason for pacifism.*
3. **Malevolent civilization #1:** mistreats both outsiders and its own people — e.g., sends conscripts/slaves on deadly extraction runs. Hires neutral-zone mercenaries for extraction work.
4. **Malevolent civilization #2** — details TBD.
5. **Neutral "anti-culture":** a Wild West-style chaotic melting pot of exiles and people fleeing the other civilizations. No unified order; a mix of good and bad actors. Supplies mercenary bands for hire to the other civilizations.

**Key worldbuilding rules:**
- All five civilizations compete for access to the Eye's resource, including rivalries *within* the same side of the ring — not just a two-sided conflict.
- Avoiding the cliché of one half of the ring being "good" and the other "evil" — moral alignment is mixed on both halves.
- Proximity to a high-intensity bay roughly tracks a settlement's wealth and danger, creating a natural hierarchy of settlements beyond just the five major civilizations.

## Mercenaries & Protagonist

- Neutral-zone mercenary bands vary widely in loyalty, morals, and boundaries — some will take any job, others hold firm lines.
- **Protagonist concept:** leader of a mercenary band based in the neutral zone. Hired by both benevolent and malevolent civilizations to run extraction heists. The band leans good but works morally gray jobs and toes the line.

## Open Threads

- What makes the Eye's mineral deposit so valuable.
- The monk civilization's reason for pacifism.
- What stabilizes the moon's pole-to-pole orbit.
- Whether the Anti-Eye gets a resource of its own.
- Full political/geographic layout of the ring: minor settlements, and where the five civilizations and neutral zone sit relative to each other and the highest-intensity bays.

## Main Antagonist Civilization

- **Government:** theocratic. A "God Ruler" is worshipped as a literal god by the citizenry; priests function as the military/ruling class.
- **Control mechanism:** the citizenry is brainwashed into believing they are the "chosen" people. The priesthood controls the salt supply by controlling the water itself, using military ships.
- **Position on the ring:** sits at (or has claimed) the terminus with the greatest salt access of any civilization — making them the wealthiest and militarily strongest, and the primary target for raids.
- **Why they're hard to raid:** their outsized salt wealth funds a military strong enough to make direct raids difficult despite being the obvious target.

## Main Story Premise

The central plot follows the other civilizations (including the protagonist's mercenary band) working to loosen the antagonist theocracy's grip on the salt supply and free the wider ring from scarcity.

## Environment Refinement

- Large landmasses sit around the terminus (habitable ring).
- On one side of these landmasses, bodies of water form sheltered bays with less current.
- Fast-moving currents toward the Eye are comparatively few and localized, not everywhere — meaning the cult has fewer currents to monitor/control access to.

## Cult Control Mechanisms (Antagonist Civilization)

Two possible/complementary mechanisms for how the cult maintains control:

1. **Equipment monopoly:** surviving the Eye requires specialized heat-resistant equipment, which the cult confiscates/controls. Only the cult knows about the moon and its eclipse effect, which periodically makes the Eye survivable even without the equipment.
2. **Predictive authority:** the moon's eclipse is what actually makes the Eye survivable. The cult has built balloon ships capable of flying above the clouds to observe the moon directly, letting them predict safe travel windows with total accuracy — which the wider population interprets as divine insight/prophecy rather than astronomy.

These two could operate together — the cult's real edge is the predictive knowledge (option 2), while equipment confiscation (option 1) is a secondary layer of control even for those who suspect the pattern.

## Characters

**Main Protagonist**
- Former low-level apostate cult member.
- Motivation: a mix of feeling alive through risky adventure, revenge, and spiteful/sabotage-driven humor.
- Background: raised within the cult, experienced it as repressive. Was seldom approved for seafaring or blimping — the thing he wanted most — due to his lax piety and childish pranks during voyages.
- Inciting event: his antics led him to learn forbidden information too quickly; his childhood friend died as a result, and he barely escaped himself. He carries guilt over his friend's death.
- Current constraint: his ability to act on his desire for revenge is stifled because open action would bring consequences down on his own family and his late friend's family, who remain devoutly pious.
- Coping mechanism: distracts himself from his misery through ongoing childish pranks on cult bureaucrats, while quietly plotting for a way to strike a real blow.

**The Scientist**
- Story's inciting driver. Has deduced the existence of the moon from tidal frequency patterns, and hypothesizes that travel to the Eye should be safe during eclipses.
- Hires the protagonist to help test/act on this theory.
- Has a hidden motivation, not yet resolved — could mark him as antagonistic, or simply as someone whose true motives need to be reconciled with the protagonist's before they can genuinely work together.
