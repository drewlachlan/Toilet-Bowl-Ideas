# The Scientist

## Background

An older man, never highly ranked within the cult hierarchy. Decades ago, he deduced the
moon's existence from patterns in tidal frequency and worked out that its eclipse
periodically makes the Eye survivable. He brought this discovery to the cult, believing
it would earn him accolades and lead to wider, more equitable access to salt across the
ring.

Instead, the cult seized on the discovery and turned it into the foundation of their
control — using his science to construct the "divine prediction" that convinced the
wider population the priesthood was chosen by god. He got none of the credit or good
he'd hoped for. What he got was regret.

## Captivity

Rather than being cast out or imprisoned outright, he has spent years living in gilded
captivity — kept in comfort and opulence by the cult, a reward calculated to keep his
conscience quiet. For a long time, it worked. But as the years pass, the opulence loses
its appeal, and he has found ways to smuggle in news of the outside world. What he
learns disturbs him — seeing what his discovery became, and what it costs the people
living under it.

## Meeting the Protagonist

He is freed, incidentally, during one of the protagonist's pranks/sabotage against the
cult. He attaches himself to the protagonist's crew, but does not reveal who he is or
what he did.

**Why he hides it:**
- He believes that if the truth comes out, he'll likely be killed by people oppressed by
  the cult he helped create.
- His deepest fear isn't the cult's retaliation — it's the protagonist specifically. He
  knows the protagonist's severe hatred for the cult, and fears that hatred turned on him
  personally.

## Motivation

To end the cult, as a way of soothing his conscience and making some kind of amends for
what he set in motion. He genuinely joins the protagonist's cause — this part is not a
lie.

## The Central Tension

As the two men work together, a real friendship forms. This becomes the trap he built
for himself: the more genuine the bond grows, the more unbearable the deception becomes.
He isn't hiding from justice so much as hiding from the good opinion of someone he's
come to care about — which makes the secret harder to hold onto the longer it goes on,
not easier.

Eventually, keeping the secret risks the protagonist's life directly, forcing the reveal
rather than allowing a clean, voluntary confession. He doesn't get to choose the moral
high ground — he's cornered into honesty, consistent with a man who's spent years
finding comfortable ways not to act.

**Open question:** what exactly threatens to kill the protagonist if the secret stays
buried —
- *Operational version:* the scientist holds specific knowledge about the cult's
  defenses/patterns that the protagonist needs, and he can't share it without revealing
  how he knows it. (Keeps the information-control theme central through the climax.)
- *Personal version:* the cult has identified his location/proximity to the protagonist
  and is closing in; warning him means outing himself. (More intimate and immediate.)

## Character Touchstones

Loosely modeled on a blend of:
- **Horace Slughorn** (regret over a past mistake whose consequences he never intended,
  softened for years by comfort and denial)
- **Nathan Rahl**-style captivity (years of gilded confinement used to buy compliance and
  silence)
